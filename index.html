Different priorities may create tension within the same organization

Departments can support the same organizational success while emphasizing different outcomes. The examples below illustrate possible tensions and practical ways an organization might manage them; they are not fixed rules.

Possible examples of conflict

1
Sales and Finance

For example, Sales may favor a discount to win an order, while Finance may worry about the lower profit margin.

2
Warehousing and Finance

For example, Warehousing may prefer more safety stock, while Finance may seek lower inventory and working-capital costs.

3
Product Management and Procurement

For example, Product Management may request more variety, while Procurement may prefer standardization and fewer suppliers.

Possible recommendations

1
Use shared goals

Consider evaluating important decisions against overall organizational performance, not only one department’s local target.

2
Discuss decisions cross-functionally

Consider involving affected departments early so that information, constraints, and priorities are visible before a decision is made.

3
Clarify trade-offs and authority

It may help to define decision criteria, responsibilities, and escalation rules before disagreements occur.
